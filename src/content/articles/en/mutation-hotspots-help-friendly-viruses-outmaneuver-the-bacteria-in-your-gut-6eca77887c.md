Phages are viruses that infect bacteria, and their arms race with bacteria drives evolution on both sides. Researchers at Michigan State University found that the genome of Enterobacteria phage T2 contains mutation hotspots called contingency loci, which mutate thousands of times faster than the rest of the genome and help the phages evade bacterial defense systems.

In experiments, the researchers transferred cholera DNA encoding a bacterial defense system into E. coli and exposed the bacteria to phages. Within a few hours, the phages consistently developed resistance to the host's defenses. This rapid adaptation was linked to mutations in a gene called agt, particularly in a repetitive DNA region where nucleotide bases repeat; these mutations caused some phages to gain or lose repeat units, altering gene reading.

Bioinformatic analysis led by Jeffrey Barrick found hundreds of similar mutation hotspots across the genomes of other phage species. While the researchers suggest potential applications for phage therapy, the biological significance of these hotspots is not yet demonstrated and further work is needed.
