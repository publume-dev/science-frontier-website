Hessian fly larvae cause about a 5% reduction in U.S. wheat yield annually, making them a significant agricultural pest.

After a 50-year search, researchers cloned the wheat gene H13 that confers resistance to Hessian fly larvae. The gene directly interacts with a specific protein in the larval saliva, triggering an immune response. This response involves cell death, cell wall reinforcement, and production of toxic molecules. The research was published in the journal Science Advances on August 14, 2026.

This discovery provides new insights into the molecular interactions between plants and pests, and could potentially be applied to develop more effective insect-resistant crop varieties.
