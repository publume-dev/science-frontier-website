In repeating partial tidal disruption events (rpTDEs), a star loses material each time it passes close to a supermassive black hole but is not completely destroyed. Among roughly 10 such systems identified so far, four have shown flares that become progressively fainter, a pattern lacking a theoretical explanation.

A team led by doctoral student Ananya Bandopadhyay at Syracuse University, with postdoctoral researcher Benjamin Amend and associate professor Eric Coughlin, published simulations in The Astrophysical Journal proposing that if a star was already rotating rapidly before its first encounter—due to the Hills mechanism, where a binary system is torn apart by the black hole—later tidal interactions cannot spin it up much further. This causes the peak fallback rate to decrease, making each flare fainter. The mechanism also explains the stars' rapid rotation and tight orbits.

The theory offers a self-consistent physical explanation for the fading of rpTDEs, but the simulations have not yet been directly compared with specific observed fading events, and the small sample size limits statistical strength. The speculated link to Sagittarius A* in the Milky Way requires further observational evidence.
