Smell-based navigation is difficult to study because wind carries odors through turbulence rather than along smooth gradients. To examine this motion, the research combined computer simulations with laboratory experiments, using laser visualization of odor plumes to measure how air and odor move together. The work is part of the interdisciplinary Odor2Action network, which involves scientists from 16 institutions.

In the PRX Life paper, the researchers report that turbulence transforms odor signals through three systematic effects: some frequencies are filtered out, others are spread around, and new frequencies are generated.

The researchers hypothesize that these systematic frequency changes help animals determine the distance and direction to an odor source.
