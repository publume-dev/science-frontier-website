Industrial hydrogen peroxide production typically demands high energy input and complex catalytic systems, whereas the new system operates at ambient conditions using inexpensive, abundant materials.

The hybrid material combines bismuth oxychloride with a purple membrane from archaea to produce hydrogen peroxide from sunlight, air, and water, achieving more than five times the yield of the semiconductor alone. In parallel, the system converts ethylene glycol into more valuable products, including glycolaldehyde, glyoxal, and formic acid.

Although promising, the report lacks quantitative details on yield, efficiency, stability, and conversion rates, so the practical applicability of the system remains to be demonstrated.
