According to Sholto David, at least 54 papers on cell aging have apparently used an antibody that targets bacterial β-galactosidase instead of the mammalian version. The issue came to light when a researcher sent David a paper in Cell that listed an E. coli β-galactosidase antibody used in mouse liver tissue.

Retired cancer geneticist Dan Liebermann acknowledged mistakenly using the E. coli antibody instead of the mammalian one in a 2010 Cancer Research paper on cellular stress responses in breast cancer.

Aled Edwards, a biochemist at the Structural Genome Consortium, says the issue highlights a larger problem in biology where reagents are used poorly or not adequately characterized. It is not clear whether the flaws compromise the studies; Sholto David says they might in some cases.
