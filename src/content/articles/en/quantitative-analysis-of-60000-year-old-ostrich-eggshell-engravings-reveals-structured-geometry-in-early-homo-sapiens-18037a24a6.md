The material comes from 112 engraved ostrich eggshell fragments recovered at three southern African sites: Diepkloof, Klipdrift, and Apollo 11.

A systematic quantitative analysis found that more than 80% of the configurations contained consistent spatial patterns. Angles close to 90° and sets of parallel lines recurred, while the more elaborate designs combined rotation, translation, repetition, and embedding.

The researchers interpret the structured geometric organization as evidence of visuo-spatial planning and emerging abstract thought in Homo sapiens around 60,000 years ago. The report does not discuss limitations, such as potential subjectivity in motif classification or the representativeness of the sample, so the strength of that conclusion is not fully assessed.
