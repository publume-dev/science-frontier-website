In November 2022, Oregon voters passed Measure 114, which would require a permit to purchase firearms and ban magazines holding more than ten rounds. The measure passed by 24,971 votes, a 1.3-percentage-point margin, with 975,862 votes in favor and 950,891 against. A study in the Journal of Public Economics examined whether the referendum itself changed gun-buying behavior.

The study found that background checks for firearm purchases in Oregon rose nearly 14% in the run-up to the election and surged 157% after the measure passed. Researchers estimated that 18 months after the election, Oregon had about 63,000 additional background checks compared with what would be expected without the measure. The increase was driven largely by new buyers rather than people who had already planned to buy acting sooner, according to the researchers.

The authors caution that the exact number of additional firearms is unknowable: Oregon has no firearm registry, background checks do not always result in purchases, and one check can cover multiple guns. They also note that even if Measure 114 is eventually implemented, it would take more than a year for Oregon's gun stock to return to the level expected without the referendum, a timescale that depends on unresolved legal challenges.
