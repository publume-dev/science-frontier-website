GRCop-42, an alloy of copper, chromium, and niobium developed by NASA, has high thermal conductivity and maintains strength at extreme temperatures, making it suitable for aerospace applications like liquid rocket engine combustion chambers. However, its 3D-printing parameter space is vast, with over 100 million possible combinations, making traditional trial-and-error prohibitively expensive.

The researchers trained an AI model on data from 37 previously failed printing configurations. The model then recommended groups of new configurations to test, balancing promising settings and unexplored regions to improve the model. The team limited the project to 40 experiments total over three months, and successfully printed GRCop-42 using 500W laser power for the first time, identifying six successful configurations overall.

The success at 500W suggests the alloy could be printed on more common commercial equipment, potentially reducing costs. The AI-guided approach could also be adapted to identify processing conditions for other alloys and additive manufacturing systems, and more broadly to problems where successful experiments are rare and testing all possibilities is prohibitively expensive.
