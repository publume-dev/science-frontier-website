Greenland sharks can live up to 400 years, making them the longest-living vertebrates known to science.

Researchers found no evidence of cell death in the retinas of Greenland shark eyes, contrary to expectations for such old animals. The rhodopsin protein in their retinas remains active and is tuned to detect blue light, an adaptation to low-light Arctic waters. These findings suggest a DNA repair mechanism may allow Greenland sharks to preserve eyesight for centuries without retinal degeneration.

The study reveals a remarkable resilience in the eye cells of Greenland sharks, implying that their longevity may come with mechanisms to maintain tissue function. However, the detailed experimental procedures and statistics from the original study are not directly available in the report, so the full extent of these conclusions remains to be verified.
