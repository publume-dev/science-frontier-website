Traditional MXene production relies on liquid-phase chemical etching, which typically takes 12–24 hours and generates toxic hydrogen fluoride waste. This time-consuming and environmentally hazardous process limits the scalability and application of MXenes.

Researchers at Rice University developed an ultrafast synthesis method that uses flash Joule heating with gas-phase etching to produce MXenes in less than 30 seconds. The process employs a combination of chlorine gas and tetrafluoromethane gas to selectively remove aluminum layers from MAX phases, converting them into MXenes, thus avoiding the long duration and toxic waste of traditional liquid-phase etching.

This advance reduces MXene manufacturing time from hours to under 30 seconds while avoiding toxic byproducts such as hydrogen fluoride, offering a faster and more environmentally friendly route. However, the specific performance parameters and scalability of the new method remain to be further validated.
