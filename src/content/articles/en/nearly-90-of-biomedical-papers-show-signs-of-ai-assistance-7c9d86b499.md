The use of AI tools in academic publishing has been increasing, but previous estimates of AI-assisted writing may have been too low. A new preprint applies a more sensitive method to reassess the prevalence of AI use in biomedical papers.

The preprint estimates that almost 90% of biomedical papers published in December 2025 in PubMed Central showed signs of AI-assisted writing, with usage rates of 77% for all 2025 papers and 52% for 2024 papers. The method is more sensitive than previous approaches, raising the estimated LLM use rate for 2024 abstracts from 13.5% to 31%. LLM use was more frequent in abstracts, introductions, and discussion sections than in methods and results sections; in December 2025 papers, 78% of discussion sections and 58% of results sections showed signs of AI.

These higher estimates are consistent with a 2025 survey in which 71% of researchers said they use AI for writing assistance. However, the study is a preprint and has not yet been peer reviewed, and it only included papers written in English, so it may not be representative of the entire scientific literature.
