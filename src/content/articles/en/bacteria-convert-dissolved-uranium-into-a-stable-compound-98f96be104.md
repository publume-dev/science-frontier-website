Pentavalent uranium has previously been considered unstable and short-lived. A 2020 study of soil samples from Croatia first demonstrated the compound FeU(V)O4, but until now it was unknown whether bacteria play a role in its formation.

In experiments with glycerol provided as a food source, bacteria transformed uranium dissolved in water; after 130 days, only around 5 percent of the uranium remained in the samples. The researchers confirmed that uranium had accumulated within the bacteria's cell walls and identified an unusually high proportion of pentavalent uranium in the analyzed biomass. That pentavalent uranium combined with iron and oxygen to form FeU(V)O4, which remained stable even when exposed to oxygen, with its amount increasing rather than decreasing.

The finding identifies bacteria as key to forming a stable uranium compound, but the practical applicability of bacteria-based uranium remediation remains unresolved; the researchers state further investigation is needed.
