An international team led by biologists at Heinrich Heine University (HHU) reconstructed 420 metabolic reactions common to all life and found that the last universal common ancestor (LUCA) possessed enzymes for only about half, with the other half catalyzed by metals in the environment.

The team found that ancestors of bacteria and archaea independently evolved structurally distinct enzymes to catalyze the same essential metabolic reactions, suggesting they transitioned to a free-living state separately. They also identified a potential pre-ATP energy source: phosphite, naturally found in hydrothermal vents, reacts with organic compounds in the presence of palladium, driving metabolic phosphorylation reactions in water overnight. Additionally, they developed a method to order the 420 reactions from simplest to most complex, with proof that a unique order exists, which may reflect the order of appearance in early evolution.

These findings support the possibility that life had two independent origins and provide a new energy source clue for how early metabolism arose. However, uncertainties remain regarding the study's assumptions and interpretations.
