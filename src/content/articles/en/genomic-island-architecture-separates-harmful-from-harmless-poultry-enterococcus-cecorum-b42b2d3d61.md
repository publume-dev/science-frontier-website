Enterococcus cecorum, a bacterium associated with poultry, has both nonpathogenic and disease-causing strains. The study compared 145 genomes: 95 from nonpathogenic strains and 50 from pathogenic strains.

Using machine learning on the architecture of genomic-island cassettes, the researchers distinguished disease-causing strains from nonpathogenic ones. Disease-causing strains were more likely to contain genomic islands enriched with genes for antibiotic resistance and for the movement of genetic material between bacteria. The article does not report quantitative performance metrics such as accuracy or sensitivity, so the strength of the classification remains unquantified.

The authors present the method as a research tool for future studies, not as a diagnostic, and say it needs additional validation before use in routine surveillance.
