X-ray flashes are brief bursts of high-energy radiation from stellar explosions, often difficult to catch. On August 27, 2025, the Einstein Probe satellite detected such a flash, designated EP250827b.

Just 5.5 hours later, the Zwicky Transient Facility found the optical counterpart, supernova SN 2025wkm, and classified it as a Type Ic-BL supernova, lacking hydrogen and helium with extremely fast-moving ejecta. Its bolometric light curve showed a plateau lasting about 20 days, which cannot be explained by radioactive decay alone—the first such plateau seen in an X-ray flash supernova. Modeling suggests a newborn magnetar with a magnetic field of about 5 × 10^14 gauss and a spin period of 1.9 milliseconds powered the extra energy.

This observation directly supports magnetars as an energy source for supernovae, though the report lacks absolute magnitude and distance, which could affect further physical interpretation.
