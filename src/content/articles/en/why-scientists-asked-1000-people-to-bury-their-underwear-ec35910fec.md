In a Swiss citizen science project, 1,000 volunteers buried over 2,000 pairs of cotton underwear and 12,000 tea bags at about 1,000 sites across Switzerland. The decomposition rate of the underwear served as an indicator of soil biological activity.

The study found that cotton underwear decomposed at different speeds depending on location, with private gardens showing the fastest decomposition and lawns the lowest; agricultural fields and meadows ranked in between. Among the factors studied, land use had the strongest influence on decomposition, explaining differences better than most soil characteristics measured, and chemical properties such as nutrient levels also significantly affected soil activity.

The researchers suggest creating an 'underwear index' to help the public understand soil processes. The project produced one of Switzerland's most comprehensive datasets on soil life, with about 240 citizen scientists listed as co-authors.
