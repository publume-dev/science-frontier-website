Topological insulators are quantum materials with distinctive electronic properties. Spin-orbit coupling strength has generally been considered an intrinsic fixed property of a material, with little attention paid to its variation with environment.

In simulations, the authors observed that raising temperature weakens spin-orbit coupling in Bi2Se3, driving a switch from a topological insulator to a normal insulator. Over a temperature range of 0–900 K, the reduction in spin-orbit coupling reached up to 0.19 eV, suppressing the band inversion needed for topological order. Topologically trivial structures appeared above 500 K, and their population increased with higher temperatures. Lattice expansion alone could not explain the transition; the temperature-induced weakening of spin-orbit coupling was identified as the primary driver. The effect was also confirmed in Bi2Te3 and Sb2Te3, where spin-orbit coupling likewise weakened as temperature rose.

These findings suggest spin-orbit coupling is not an immutable quantity, but might act as a tuning knob for electronic properties, with potential implications for spintronics and quantum computing. However, because the work is simulation-based, experimental verification at elevated temperatures is not yet available.
