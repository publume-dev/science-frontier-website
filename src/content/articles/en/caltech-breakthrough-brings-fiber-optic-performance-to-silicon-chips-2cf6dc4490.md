Traditionally, light transmission on silicon chips relies on materials like silicon nitride, which suffer from significant signal loss at visible wavelengths.

Caltech researchers developed a method to print germano-silicate glass waveguides directly onto 8- and 12-inch silicon wafers, achieving ultralow light loss comparable to optical fiber. At visible wavelengths, this platform exceeds the performance of silicon nitride by a factor of 20 in reducing signal loss. The low melting temperature of germano-silicate allows a 'reflow' process that smooths waveguide surfaces to atomic level, suppressing scattering loss. Lasers built with the new platform show more than a 100-fold improvement in coherence time over previous designs.

This advance could bring a new level of performance to silicon photonics, though the improvements are measured under specific conditions and other factors may affect real-world deployment.
