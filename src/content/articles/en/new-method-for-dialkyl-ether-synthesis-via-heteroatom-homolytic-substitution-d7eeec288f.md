Traditional dialkyl ether synthesis typically relies on SN2 or SN1 reactions, which are often inefficient or inapplicable for sterically hindered substrates such as coupling tertiary alcohols with secondary or primary alcohols. This research aimed to develop a new synthetic strategy to address this long-standing challenge.

The researchers report a radical-based method for dialkyl ether synthesis via heteroatom homolytic substitution (het-SH2), coupling carboxylic acid-derived redox-active esters with alcohols using a titanium catalyst and visible-light photoredox catalysis. This method enables efficient synthesis of dialkyl ethers with 3°–2°, 3°–1°, 2°–2°, and 2°–1° substitution patterns, overcoming the steric limitations of classical SN2 and SN1 reactions.

The approach provides access to sterically demanding BCP ether bioisosteres and allows late-stage diversification of complex pharmaceutical scaffolds, offering potential value for drug discovery.
