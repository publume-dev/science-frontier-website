Arctic permafrost stores an estimated 1.9 trillion tons (1.7 trillion metric tons) of organic carbon, nearly twice the amount currently in Earth's atmosphere. Thawing permafrost could release this carbon, affecting climate.

A NASA-led study developed the AI framework GeoCryoAI, which combines satellite observations, model outputs, and field measurements dating back to 1891, to produce high-resolution maps of the Arctic zero-curtain phenomenon. The maps show that spring thaw generally produces much longer zero-curtain periods than autumn freeze-up, and that regions with higher moisture levels experience longer zero-curtain periods.

Understanding the zero curtain will help predict permafrost thaw's climate effects, but the article does not provide quantitative estimates of greenhouse gas emissions from zero-curtain extension.
