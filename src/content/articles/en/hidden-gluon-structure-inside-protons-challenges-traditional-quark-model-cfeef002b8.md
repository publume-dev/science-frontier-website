The internal structure of the proton has long been a key topic in physics. The standard picture holds that baryon number is carried by the proton's valence quarks, while gluons mediate the strong force. A recent study, however, challenges this view.

Data from the STAR detector at RHIC suggest that baryon number may be carried by a Y-shaped gluon junction rather than solely by valence quarks. The researchers observed roughly twice as many baryons emerging perpendicular to the beamline as expected from the electric charge of stopped quarks, indicating an additional source of baryon number. The gluon junction concept was first proposed in the 1970s and further developed by Kharzeev in 1996. At high energies, gluons multiply and split, reducing the momentum per gluon, which may make the junction easier to stop in collisions.

If confirmed, this finding would imply that the standard quark model needs revision, as gluon junctions may carry baryon number, potentially rewriting textbook explanations of proton structure. However, the results come from a single study and require independent confirmation from other experiments.
