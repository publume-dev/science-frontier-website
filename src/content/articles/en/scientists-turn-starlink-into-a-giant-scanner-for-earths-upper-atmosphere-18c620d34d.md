The thermosphere is a layer of Earth's upper atmosphere where density variations affect satellite drag. Previous efforts typically relied on measurements along the paths of dedicated satellites, limiting knowledge of global two-dimensional density patterns.

The Kyoto team applied tomography to publicly available orbital data from roughly 1,200 Starlink satellites flying at an altitude of 482 kilometers, estimating thermospheric density. This produced a two-dimensional latitude-longitude snapshot of density at about 500 kilometers altitude, marking the first tomographic analysis of its kind. The work expands on the team's earlier study that relied on Two-Line Element data to track density changes over time and altitude, adding horizontal variation across latitude and longitude.

The resulting density patterns closely matched observations from the European Space Agency's SWARM satellites, which measure atmospheric density along their orbits, indicating the approach is promising for monitoring thermospheric density. However, the report does not specify the exact quantitative agreement with SWARM data or provide error estimates for the density measurements.
