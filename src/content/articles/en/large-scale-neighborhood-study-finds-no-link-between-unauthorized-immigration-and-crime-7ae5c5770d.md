Past research on the relationship between unauthorized immigration and crime has only examined state and metropolitan-area levels. A new peer-reviewed study extends this question to the neighborhood level for the first time on a large national scale.

The researchers tracked crime trends in 11,500 neighborhoods representing 46 million residents across diverse U.S. cities from 2010 to 2018. To estimate undocumented populations at the neighborhood level, they used restricted individual-level Census data accessed through a federal Research Data Center and an imputation method similar to one used by the Department of Homeland Security. Neighborhoods where the share of undocumented residents grew showed larger reductions in property crime and no significant change in violent crime.

The results were not uniform: aggravated assaults declined significantly in those neighborhoods, but robberies increased. The researchers cannot definitively confirm that the robbery increase reflects a 'Walking ATM' pattern of victimization because the data do not identify the immigration status of crime victims.
