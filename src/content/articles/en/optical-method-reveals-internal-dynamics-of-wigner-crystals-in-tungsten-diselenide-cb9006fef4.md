Wigner crystals are ordered electron lattices. Wigner crystal polarons are hybrid quasiparticles that form when excitons couple to that ordered lattice, and they act as optical probes of the crystal and its collective dynamics.

In the new experiments, researchers at the University of Basel and the Technical University of Munich cooled a single layer of tungsten diselenide to a few degrees above absolute zero, illuminated it, and recorded the reflected light. The measurements revealed new optical features attributed to Wigner crystal polarons. The team found that the strength of electron–electron interactions shapes these signatures, and a TUM-developed theoretical model explains how the polarons emerge from coupling between excitons and the crystal's collective electron motion.

These optical signatures provide a way to observe the internal dynamics of a Wigner crystal through light. The available report, however, gives no quantitative values, measurement uncertainties, or replication status for the signatures, and it does not describe method limitations beyond the general difficulty of probing such states.
