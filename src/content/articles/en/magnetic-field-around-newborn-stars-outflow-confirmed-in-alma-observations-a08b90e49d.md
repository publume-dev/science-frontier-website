The predicted toroidal magnetic-field geometry around protostellar outflows — a twisted, funnel-shaped field wrapped around the escaping gas — had not been directly confirmed at high resolution. ALMA observations of the young star system NGC 1333 IRAS 4A now provide that confirmation.

Using ALMA at roughly 30 times sharper resolution than earlier telescopes, the team traced magnetic fields through the polarization of carbon monoxide emission at distances of a few hundred astronomical units from the protostar. They measured a field strength of a few thousandths of a gauss; the field ran perpendicular to the gas flow and matched the outflow's rotation.

The researchers derived a linear relationship, based on Ampère's law, between magnetic-field twisting and electric currents, which provides a direct way to map magnetic fields in star-forming regions. The findings appear in Nature Communications (DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-75950-5).
