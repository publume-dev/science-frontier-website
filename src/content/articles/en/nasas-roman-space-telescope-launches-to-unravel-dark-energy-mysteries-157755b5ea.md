Roman's primary instrument is a 300-megapixel infrared camera with a field of view about 100 times larger than Hubble's. The telescope will survey the universe about 1,000 times faster than Hubble and map 20 billion stars in the Milky Way. Its primary mirror measures 2.4 meters, originally designed for a spy satellite and donated by the National Reconnaissance Office.

Roman launched on August 31, 2026, aboard a Falcon Heavy rocket from Kennedy Space Center, targeting L2 about one million miles from Earth. Its Coronagraph Instrument will directly image Jupiter-sized exoplanets, demonstrating technology for future missions like the Habitable Worlds Observatory. First images are expected in early 2027.

Roman's core goal is to probe dark energy, and its wide field and rapid survey capabilities promise new cosmological insights. Although the reported launch time differs slightly between sources (7:26 vs 7:35 a.m. EDT), the successful launch is confirmed. Future data releases will test its scientific yield.
