On Earth, the magnetospheric cusp—the funnel-shaped region where solar wind plasma can enter the magnetic field—is located near noon local time. At Saturn, two features complicate that picture: the planet rotates about once every 10.7 hours, and it emits abundant ionized material from the moon Enceladus.

Analyzing Cassini observations collected from 2004 to 2010, researchers found that Saturn's cusp is usually located between 13:00 and 15:00 local time and sometimes extends toward 20:00, rather than near noon as on Earth. They attribute the duskward displacement to the planet's rapid rotation and the abundant ionized material supplied by Enceladus, supporting the idea that rotation can dominate over the solar wind in shaping a magnetosphere.

The afternoon cusp location changes how Saturn's bright auroras are interpreted and where magnetic reconnection and high-energy particle acceleration are expected.
