Atmospheric carbon dioxide has risen from about 369 parts per million (ppm) in 2000 to more than 420 ppm today. Blood chemistry, including bicarbonate, helps maintain acid-base balance, yet long-term trends in healthy populations have been unclear.

Using NHANES data from about 7,000 people at two-year intervals from 1999 to 2020, researchers found that average serum bicarbonate increased by about 7% since 1999, while average blood calcium and phosphorus decreased. Modeling indicates that if current trends continue, average bicarbonate could approach the upper limit of today's accepted healthy range within 50 years.

The authors state the study does not establish a direct cause-and-effect relationship between atmospheric CO2 and the observed blood chemistry changes. The correlations might be influenced by diet, medication, or other environmental factors, so these results should be interpreted as associative evidence rather than proof of causation.
