Silicate mineral weathering is a natural but slow process. Traditional methods may rely on chemical acids or slow natural reactions over long timescales.

Researchers engineered Gluconobacter oxydans bacteria to accelerate the weathering of olivine and enstatite. This process releases nickel and cobalt and forms magnesium oxalate. Over 15 days, the bacteria extracted up to 75% of magnesium from olivine samples.

Magnesium oxalate can store twice as much carbon per magnesium atom as magnesite, a commonly studied carbon-storage mineral, making the microbial method potentially advantageous for carbon capture. However, the article does not provide comparison data with synthetic acids or cell-free solutions for dissolution rates, so relative efficiency remains unclear.
