Correct assembly of outer membrane proteins is essential for bacterial survival and virulence, a process coordinated by the BAM complex and the chaperone SurA. The precise mechanism by which SurA delivers substrates to BAM was previously unclear, limiting understanding of the process and the development of potential antibiotic targets.

Using cryo-EM and disulfide-bond mutations to stabilize different SurA conformations, researchers captured four structural snapshots. The structures show that during interaction with BAM, SurA's Core domain moves progressively closer to the complex, while the P1 and P2 domains adopt distinct conformations. Notably, the P2 domain interacts with the BAM component BamE, and disrupting this interaction reduced outer membrane protein assembly.

These snapshots reveal key steps in the dynamic interplay between SurA and BAM, providing a foundation for understanding outer membrane protein assembly. Although the importance of the P2-BamE interaction is established, the report does not provide the magnitude or quantitative effect on assembly efficiency, leaving the practical impact uncertain.
