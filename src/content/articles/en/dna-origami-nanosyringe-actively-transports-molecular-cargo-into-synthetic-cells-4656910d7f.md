In synthetic cells, molecular cargo needs to cross a lipid membrane before it can take part in reactions inside the compartment. To provide that access, researchers at the University of Stuttgart built a programmable DNA-origami nanosyringe for active membrane transport.

Driven by reversible mechanical motion, the nanosyringe moved cargo across the membrane and into synthetic cells. The team used it to spatially initiate DNA hybridization chain reactions, to activate RNA transcription through targeted delivery of promoter activators, and to introduce catalytic DNAzymes that cleaved RNA substrates inside membrane-bound compartments.

Reported in Nature Nanotechnology, the study demonstrates a mechanical principle for programmable DNA nanodevices and active control of membrane transport in space and time. Because the reported outcomes are qualitative and include no efficiency or rate measurements, the magnitude of these effects remains unspecified.
