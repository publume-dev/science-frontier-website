Converting carbon dioxide into fuels using sunlight is appealing, but common photoelectrode materials such as cuprous oxide degrade in aqueous environments, limiting their long-term use.

The team built a photocathode with a nano-sandwich structure combining cuprous oxide, MXene, and an ultrathin titanium dioxide layer formed by controlled heating. In laboratory tests, the system reached a solar-to-ethanol conversion efficiency of 2.74%, with ethanol as the sole liquid product rather than a mixture of byproducts. The design also addressed the long-standing degradation issue of cuprous oxide, maintaining activity over many hours. The paper was published in Advanced Energy Materials (DOI: 10.1002/aenm.71469) by Luis A. M. Carrascosa et al.

The advance shows that carefully designed nanostructures can improve both stability and selectivity of photoelectrodes, but the magnitude of the efficiency improvement is unclear because the article does not provide a baseline comparison to previous systems. Long-term stability is also not precisely quantified, warranting further investigation.
