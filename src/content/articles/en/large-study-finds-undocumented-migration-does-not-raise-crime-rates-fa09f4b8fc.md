Debates about undocumented immigration and crime often lack large-scale data. This study analyzed 11,500 neighborhoods across more than 100 US cities from 2010 to 2018, combining estimates of undocumented residents with crime data.

The researchers used non-public demographic data for 46 million people from a US federal statistical research data center. They found that increases in undocumented immigrant populations were associated with lower levels of property crimes and aggravated assaults, but robberies tended to rise. On average, undocumented residents made up 5.6% of a neighborhood's population in 2010, dropping to 4.8% in 2018. The paper was published in the Journal of Urban Affairs on 10 August.

The study offers new large-scale evidence that undocumented immigration does not increase overall crime rates, with some crime categories actually declining. However, because it is observational, it cannot establish causation; the authors suggest that the rise in robberies might be due to immigrants being victims, not offenders. Estimates of undocumented populations are inherently uncertain due to indirect methods.
