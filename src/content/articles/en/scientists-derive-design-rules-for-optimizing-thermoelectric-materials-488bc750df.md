Thermoelectric materials convert heat into electricity directly, and their performance is often quantified by parameters such as the Seebeck coefficient. Optimizing the band structure is a key strategy for improving thermoelectric performance, but systematic theoretical guidance has been lacking.

Using Boltzmann transport theory, the researchers derived design rules for optimizing the band structure of thermoelectric materials. A central finding is that the bipolar effect becomes significant when the thermal energy reaches about five times the band gap. This rule provides a quantitative criterion for materials design.

The study offers a new theoretical framework for designing thermoelectric materials, enabling more targeted band-structure optimization. However, the article does not specify the exact conditions for optimal band convergence or the precise chemical potential values, as these are only qualitatively described; practical application will require validation in specific material systems.
