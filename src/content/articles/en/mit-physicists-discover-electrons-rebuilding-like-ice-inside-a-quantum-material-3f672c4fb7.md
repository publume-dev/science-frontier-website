In the quantum material erbium tritelluride, electrons spontaneously form a checkerboard pattern known as a charge density wave. The dominant charge density wave appears below -8 degrees Celsius, while a subdominant one appears below -113 degrees Celsius.

The researchers used two laser pulses: the first to disrupt the electronic checkerboard and the second to measure the recovery via photoemission. They found that the dominant charge density wave recovers gradually and uniformly, consistent with a second-order transition, while the subdominant one returns through scattered pockets that expand outward, indicating a first-order transition.

The findings could help understand more complex materials with multiple coexisting electronic phases, such as high-temperature superconductors.
