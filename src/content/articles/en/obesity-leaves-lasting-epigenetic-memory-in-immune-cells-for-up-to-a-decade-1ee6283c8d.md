Obesity is known to raise the risk of various diseases, but whether weight loss quickly reverses that risk has been unclear. Research published in EMBO Reports set out to test whether obesity leaves a long-term molecular imprint on immune cells.

The team analyzed immune cells from four distinct human groups—weight-loss injection patients, Alstrom syndrome patients, exercise intervention participants, and osteoarthritis surgery cohorts—plus mouse models, to examine how obesity alters DNA methylation in CD4+ helper T cells. They found that these cells retain DNA methylation changes tied to past obesity, linked to autophagy and immune senescence pathways, and that the changes were still detectable 5 to 10 years after weight loss.

The authors suggest that short-term weight loss may not immediately lower the risk of type 2 diabetes and some cancers, and that sustained weight management over several years may be needed for obesity-related T cell changes to fade. They also propose that drugs such as SGLT2 inhibitors could potentially be repurposed to speed up the reversal of these immune changes, though this remains an exploratory idea.
