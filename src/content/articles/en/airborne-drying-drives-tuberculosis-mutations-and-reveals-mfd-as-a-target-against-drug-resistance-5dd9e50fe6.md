Tuberculosis, caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis, poses a global health threat, especially with the rise of drug-resistant strains. During airborne transmission, bacteria experience drying, which may stress them and alter their genetic makeup.

In a laboratory aerosolization model, researchers showed that drying induces oxidative stress and DNA damage in the bacteria, triggering a DNA repair response that promotes survival and increases mutations linked to rifampin resistance. They also found that reducing activity of the DNA repair gene Mfd impaired survival of rifampin-resistant bacteria in the aerosolization experiments.

Analysis of over 50,000 tuberculosis genomes from patients worldwide revealed that strains with Mfd mutations were significantly less likely to carry the most common rifampin-resistance mutation, suggesting Mfd is a promising target to limit resistance evolution. The study, however, did not quantify the effect size of Mfd silencing on survival nor detail the desiccation model's conditions, so the practical impact remains to be confirmed.
