Odor perception research faces challenges because chemical properties of single molecules do not always reflect their perceptual attributes, and complex mixtures add further difficulty. Previous approaches often relied on chemical descriptors such as ester class or molecular weight, but these methods have shown limited success in predicting human odor similarity.

The researchers compiled data from six datasets across three studies, creating a dataset with 168 unique single molecules, 731 unique mixtures, and 507 mixture-pair similarity measurements. They trained machine learning models to predict odor similarity using either semantic labels (e.g., 'fruity and sweet') or chemical descriptors. The final model achieved a median RMSE of 0.08 and a Pearson correlation of 0.57 on an independent validation set. Furthermore, models using semantic labels performed better than those using chemical descriptors.

This study demonstrates the potential of machine learning in predicting odor perception and suggests that semantic descriptions capture perceptual features better than chemical properties. However, the findings currently rely on a single news report and have not been validated across multiple independent datasets, so their generalizability remains to be confirmed.
