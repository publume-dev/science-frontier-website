Type 1 narcolepsy is often caused by the loss of orexin, a peptide produced by about 70,000 neurons in the hypothalamus, disrupting the sleep-wake cycle. Existing treatments have addressed symptoms rather than the underlying orexin deficiency.

Takeda conducted two phase III clinical trials enrolling 273 people with type 1 narcolepsy, comparing twice-daily oral oveporexton to placebo. Participants taking oveporexton were able to stay awake during the day and fall and stay asleep at night, and reported improvements in cataplexy, sleep paralysis, and hallucinations. The FDA approved oveporexton as the first drug targeting the root cause of narcolepsy.

The article does not report specific effect sizes or statistical significance values for the trial outcomes, and long-term safety and efficacy data are not yet available. The orexin market is projected to surpass US$6.4 billion annually by the early 2030s, indicating substantial commercial potential, but further research is needed to confirm long-term benefits.
