Solid oxide cells can either generate electricity by reducing oxygen or produce hydrogen by evolving oxygen. New measurements show that the main reaction site of a silver nanocatalyst shifts when the cell changes between these two modes.

In the oxygen-reduction (electricity-generation) mode, reaction rates increased with the length of the boundary between silver nanoparticles and the electrode, indicating the interface is the main reaction site. In the oxygen-evolution (hydrogen-production) mode, rates increased with the silver nanoparticle surface area, indicating the surface is the primary reaction site. Among the metals tested—silver, cobalt, palladium, and platinum—silver gave the strongest catalytic improvement. Synchrotron-based AP-XPS analysis and theoretical calculations also show that silver alters the electrode surface electronic structure in ways that favor oxygen reduction, while during oxygen evolution it helps oxygen atoms combine.

The silver nanocatalyst's active site is therefore not fixed; it shifts between the interface and the surface depending on the operating mode. Interpreting its catalytic performance means treating electricity generation and hydrogen production as two distinct situations rather than assuming one active site applies to all conditions.
