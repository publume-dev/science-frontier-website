For a long time, there has been debate over whether logical reasoning depends on language. A study published in PNAS by researchers at MIT's McGovern Institute directly investigated this question.

The study used non-verbal logic tasks (such as number transformation rules and geometric pattern completion) to test reasoning independently of language. Notably, participants with severe language impairments due to stroke performed these tasks as well as controls. Functional MRI scans of healthy adults showed that brain regions specialized for language were not activated during inductive or deductive reasoning. Instead, the multiple demand network—a brain system supporting complex problem-solving—was active during inductive reasoning but not during deductive reasoning.

These results suggest that logical reasoning may not depend on language and instead relies on separate neural mechanisms. However, the report lacked specific effect sizes, statistical details, and participant numbers, and it did not link directly to the PNAS paper, so the findings have not been independently verified from the primary source.
