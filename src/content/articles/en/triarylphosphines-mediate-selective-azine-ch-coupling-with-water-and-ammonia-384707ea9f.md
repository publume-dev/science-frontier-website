Azine substrates include a broad range of pyridines, as well as quinolines and diazines; in the presence of simple triarylphosphines, they undergo C–H coupling with water or ammonia.

Experimental and computational studies show that the reactions proceed through a distinct mechanism in which pendant aldehyde and imine functional groups interconvert into acetal- and aminal-type forms, leading to selective coupling and hydroxylation or amination.

Because it can act directly on complex pharmaceuticals and agrochemicals, the chemistry offers a late-stage strategy for hydroxylation or amination.
