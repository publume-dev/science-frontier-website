Bats are known to carry many dangerous viruses while rarely showing symptoms, a trait that has puzzled scientists. Conventional thinking suggested their immune systems might be unusual, but the underlying genetic details remained largely unknown.

In a study published in Science Advances, researchers from Tulane University, Stanford University, and the CDC report that vesper bats, the largest family of bats, possess two distinct copies of the genes that produce antibody heavy chains, unlike all other known mammals, which have only one. This discovery points to a previously unknown level of immune system organization in mammals.

The finding may partly explain why vesper bats are evolutionarily successful and can carry viruses without becoming ill. However, the researchers caution that it does not fully account for their role as viral reservoirs. The extra gene copies might enable a broader range of antibodies, but the exact mechanism requires further study.
