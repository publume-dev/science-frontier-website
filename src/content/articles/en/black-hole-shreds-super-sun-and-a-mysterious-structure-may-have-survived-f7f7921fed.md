AT2024wpp, nicknamed the Whippet, is a tidal disruption event in which a massive star was shredded by a black hole, releasing energy roughly 400 billion times the Sun's output, more than any known supernova. The event was detected by the Zwicky Transient Facility and confirmed within a day by the Liverpool Telescope and NASA's Swift satellite, showing characteristics of a Luminous Fast Blue Optical Transient (LFBOT).

The event generated a shock wave traveling at one-fifth the speed of light through dense surrounding gas, which fizzled out after about half a year when it reached the boundary of a bubble created by previously expelled stellar material. As the event faded, weak hydrogen and helium signatures appeared, with helium moving at over 6,000 km/s toward us, suggesting a densely bound structure may have survived the blast.

The nature of the surviving object or stream is speculative; it could be material from the star's core or a third member of the system. This discovery provides new insights into extreme astrophysical processes, but further observations are needed to confirm its true nature.
