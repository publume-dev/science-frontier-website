*Silescelida acristata* is a newly described reptile that lived about 240 million years ago, during the Middle Triassic Period. It comes from a stretch of reptile evolution that predates the appearance of dinosaurs and crocodiles.

The fossil was recovered in Dona Francisca, central Rio Grande do Sul, Brazil, inside the UNESCO Quarta Colônia Geopark. It consists mainly of limb bones, and its femur lacks a trochanter crest that is present in most close relatives. A portion of the specimen had been lost for more than twenty years before it was rediscovered in 2022 in the PUCRS scientific collection.

Phylogenetic analyses indicate that *Silescelida acristata* may belong to Euparkeriidae, a rare archosauriform group previously known mainly from Africa, Asia, and Europe. That placement is tentative because the evidence is limited to fragmentary limb bones, so the new fossil remains a possible, rather than a confirmed, South American record for the group.
