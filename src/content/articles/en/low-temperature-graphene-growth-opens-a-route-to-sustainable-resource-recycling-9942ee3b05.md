Graphene-based materials typically require high synthesis temperatures, limiting their applications. This study explores low-temperature chemical vapor deposition (CVD) using acetylene gas on a cerium oxide surface.

Adjusting the CVD temperature produced different materials: 300°C yielded graphene quantum dots, 450°C yielded aggregated graphene, and 600°C yielded porous graphene.

The method uses acetylene derived from industrial waste gases, biomass, and recycled plastics, potentially enabling sustainable recycling of high-value carbon materials.
