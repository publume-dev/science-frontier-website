GRB 061201 is a gamma-ray burst that breaks established patterns, and astronomers have been unable to identify its host galaxy, leaving its origin unexplained.

Using JWST, researchers found an extremely faint galaxy (G2) near the position of GRB 061201, with an estimated photometric redshift of about z=1.2. In contrast, a closer object (G3) has a 43% chance of being unrelated to the burst and a redshift too high to be consistent, while G2 has only an 18% chance coincidence probability.

If spectroscopically confirmed, a high-redshift origin (z~1.2) for GRB 061201 would better align with established energy patterns, afterglow behavior, and merger rates measured by gravitational-wave detectors, resolving the long-standing puzzle. However, the current redshift is based on photometric fitting, and a direct spectroscopic measurement is required to fully confirm the host galaxy identification.
