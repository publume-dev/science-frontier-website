On Wednesday, a glacial collapse in Nepal caused a massive landslide and flash flooding, killing around 350 people and leaving over 1,000 missing. The event occurred in the Himalayas, where melting glaciers and permafrost are increasing the risk of such disasters.

The ice and rock collapse generated a 5.2-magnitude seismic event and sent a massive wall of water downstream into the Bhotekoshi River system. The US Geological Survey announced that the seismic energy was generated by the landslide, not an earthquake. Satellite images from before the ice landslide did not show any major lakes forming in the area, and weather data did not record excessive rainfall preceding the landslide. Water levels on the Trishuli River, downstream of the Bhotekoshi, rose by as much as 9 metres in 30 minutes in Galchhi, according to the International Centre for Integrated Mountain Development.

Göran Ekström, a geophysicist at Columbia University, suggests that melting of glaciers and permafrost in the Himalayas probably contributed to the glacial collapse. However, the exact role of global warming in this specific event is not yet established, as scientists say it will take time to determine the extent.
