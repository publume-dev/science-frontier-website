The Murujuga rock art faces potential environmental damage, and a monitoring program has been tracking rock surface condition to assess impacts.

The third annual report found a statistically significant porosity gradient across Murujuga, with higher porosity closer to industrial sites. Multiple lines of evidence—atmospheric chemistry, rainfall pH, and laboratory testing—now point to historic levels of sulfur dioxide from industry and shipping, and the acid rain it can produce, as the most relevant factor. Sulfur dioxide emissions have fallen sharply since the 1980s, suggesting historic peaks rather than current emissions caused the increased porosity.

Although evidence supports historic sulfur emissions as the primary cause, monitoring cannot rule out any effect of current pollution, and other researchers using different methods have reached different conclusions.
