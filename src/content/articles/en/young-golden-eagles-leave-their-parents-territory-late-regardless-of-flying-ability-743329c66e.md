Young golden eagles can fly as skillfully as adults just a few months after fledging, yet many remain in their parents' territory for almost a full year afterward. A study that tracked 92 young golden eagles in the Alps with GPS transmitters over six years investigated whether the timing of departure is linked to flight skills.

The likelihood of young eagles leaving was predicted by how far they strayed and how high they soared during daily forays, not by measures of soaring skill.

The Alpine golden eagle population is saturated, with few vacant territories, which may explain why young eagles delay leaving even when capable of flight. Similar decoupling of skill acquisition and independence has been observed in New Caledonian crows and other species with long parental care. However, these findings may be specific to the saturated Alpine population and may not generalize to other golden eagle populations or species.
