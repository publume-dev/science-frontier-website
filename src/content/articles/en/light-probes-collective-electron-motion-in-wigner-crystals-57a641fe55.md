A Wigner crystal is a quantum state where electrons arrange into an ordered lattice, and its internal dynamics have been difficult to probe directly.

Researchers at the University of Basel and the Technical University of Munich developed a new method to probe how electrons move together inside a Wigner crystal using light. They studied a single atomic layer of tungsten diselenide cooled to near absolute zero and analyzed reflected light to reveal previously unseen optical features related to collective electron behavior. The signals arise from interactions between ordered electrons and excitons, forming hybrid quasiparticles called Wigner crystal polarons that act as sensitive optical probes. A theoretical team led by Professor Michael Knap at TUM developed a model explaining how these polarons form through coupling of excitons with collective electron motion.

The strength of electron interactions influences the observed optical signatures, making these signals useful for investigating strongly correlated systems and offering a new way to explore such quantum materials.
