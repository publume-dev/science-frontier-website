The anomalous Hall effect is a fundamental phenomenon in condensed matter physics. For a century, researchers assumed that observing this effect requires a magnetic field perpendicular to the plane of the film. This constraint shaped device design and limited possible architectures.

Researchers at Carnegie Mellon University demonstrated the effect experimentally by using a heterostructure of few-layered TaIrTe4 and Cr2Ge2Te6 (CGT). The proximity of CGT induces magnetic properties in TaIrTe4. Theoretical modeling by Shubhayu Chatterjee indicates that pairing with CGT reduces the material's symmetry, allowing additional spin-orbit coupling that is crucial for the in-plane anomalous Hall effect. However, the detailed mechanism remains not fully characterized, and further investigation of few-layered TaIrTe4 is needed.

The discovery could enable novel planar device architectures and sensor types. For example, it might allow vector magnetometry by measuring both out-of-plane and in-plane anomalous Hall effect signals in the same device.
