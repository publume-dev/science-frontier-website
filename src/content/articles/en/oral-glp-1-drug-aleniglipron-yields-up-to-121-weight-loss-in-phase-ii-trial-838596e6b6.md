Injectable peptide GLP-1 drugs are effective against obesity, but the article notes they may face access and manufacturing challenges. Aleniglipron is a chemically synthesized small molecule designed to be taken orally with or without food, avoiding that delivery route.

In the double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, 230 adults at 38 U.S. medical centers were randomized to daily doses of 45 mg, 90 mg, or 120 mg of aleniglipron or placebo. At 36 weeks, average weight changes were −9.0%, −10.7%, and −12.1% by dose, compared with −0.5% for placebo. Gastrointestinal side effects were mostly mild to moderate and became less frequent over time; 10.4% of participants discontinued, and no drug-induced liver injury was reported.

The pattern of greater weight loss with higher doses and mostly mild-to-moderate gastrointestinal effects must be weighed against a 10.4% discontinuation rate. Since the article does not report confidence intervals or link directly to the Nature Medicine paper, the effect sizes cannot be independently verified from this report.
