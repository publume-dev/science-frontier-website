Stomata are the leaf pores through which CO₂ enters and water escapes, so reducing their number can curb crop water loss but may also limit the CO₂ supply for photosynthesis. To see what happens inside intact leaves, researchers used high-resolution microCT at Argonne National Laboratory's Advanced Photon Source to produce three-dimensional maps of sorghum leaves, detailing how stomata connect to internal air pathways, photosynthetic tissues, and veins.

In sorghum leaves engineered to have fewer stomata, the maps showed larger air spaces directly beneath the remaining stomata. Those leaves maintained equivalent conductance of CO₂ while reducing water loss.

The maps also revealed an unusual geometry: stomata on the upper surface of sorghum leaves sat over veins rather than between them, suggesting the two leaf surfaces may contribute differently to CO₂ uptake and water loss.
