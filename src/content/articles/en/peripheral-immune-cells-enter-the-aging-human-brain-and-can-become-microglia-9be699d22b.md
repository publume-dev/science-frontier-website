Microglia are the brain's specialized immune cells, traditionally thought to originate from embryonic precursors within the brain. Using genetic mutations as markers, the researchers compared DNA from immune cells in blood and brain tissue and found that many microglia in aging humans originate from blood stem cells. Notably, this process appears unique to humans and was not observed in mice or non-human primates.

By comparing DNA markers, the study demonstrated that peripheral immune cells can enter the human brain during aging and differentiate into microglia. This mechanism was found only in humans, suggesting a human-specific pathway of aging immunity.

This finding challenges conventional views and may offer new targets for treating age-related brain diseases. However, the study does not specify the exact magnitude of immune cell infiltration, and a previous 2023 study by the same group was mentioned without details.
