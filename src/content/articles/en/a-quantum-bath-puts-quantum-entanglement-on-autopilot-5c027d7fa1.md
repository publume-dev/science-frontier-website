Entanglement is a key resource in quantum technologies, but creating and maintaining it usually requires precise active control. A theoretical proposal from over 20 years ago suggested using a “quantum bath” to autonomously generate and stabilize entanglement without ongoing intervention.

Researchers at ISTA have now demonstrated this proposal for the first time in an experiment. They used a shared source of correlated photons as a “quantum bath,” coupling the two qubits with microwave photons. The entangled state is continuously available without active control or measurement. They confirmed synchronization of the qubits using quantum tomography, with measurements lasting 20 to 80 nanoseconds.

The current method transfers only about 10% of the bath's available entanglement, indicating limited efficiency compared to active control methods. This shows that fully autonomous entanglement generation is feasible but still needs improvement.
