TIC 433545934 is a quadruple star system comprising two eclipsing binaries with inner orbital periods of 1.41 and 2.07 days, and an outer orbital period of about 224.5 days between the pairs. Previously, the candidate system KIC 5255552 was thought to show mutual eclipses between binary pairs, but lacked robust confirmation.

The team confirmed TIC 433545934 as a gravitationally bound 2+2 quadruple system via synchronized eclipse timing variations and direct observation. The system exhibits a rare 'triple-dip' eclipsing event where one binary pair passes in front of both stars of the other pair, observed only once per outer revolution and not the reverse, due to the eccentric outer orbit geometry. Additionally, the orbital planes are aligned to within about 2 degrees. This is the first confirmed 2+2 quadruple system showing both synchronized eclipse timing and mutual eclipses between the whole pairs.

This discovery provides new observational constraints on the dynamical evolution of multiple-star systems. However, uncertainties remain: stellar mass estimates are statistical only, lacking radial velocity data, and the outer orbital period relied heavily on archival sky-survey data with only a single TESS observation of the outer eclipse. Further observations are needed to refine the model.
