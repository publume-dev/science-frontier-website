Spatial transcriptomics has traditionally been limited to thin tissue sections, making it difficult to capture molecular information in three dimensions. To address this, researchers at Johns Hopkins Medicine developed 3DEEP (3D DNase-Enhanced Expression Profiling), a technology that enables spatial transcriptomics on 100–400 μm tissue sections, allowing 3D molecular snapshots of hundreds of developing hair follicles.

Using 3DEEP, the researchers ordered hair follicles by molecular age to reconstruct a 4D map of mouse hair follicle development. This map revealed three distinct phases: precursor cells establish a spatial axis, differentiate into multiple cell types, and then grow and morph into mature follicles. Comparing normal mice with hairless mice lacking the Foxn1 gene showed that hair follicles from hairless mice experienced delayed development, with cells proliferating at a higher rate but having reduced ability to mature at the right time, leading to structural collapse before hair formation.

This study demonstrates how spatial transcriptomics can be extended to three-dimensional tissues, offering new insights into organ development. However, the article does not quantify the magnitude of differences in proliferation or maturation rates between normal and Foxn1-deficient mice, nor does it specify sample sizes or statistical methods, leaving the precision and generalizability of these findings open to further verification.
