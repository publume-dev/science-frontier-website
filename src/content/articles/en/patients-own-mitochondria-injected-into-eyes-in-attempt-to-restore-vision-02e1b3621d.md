The case involved a woman with severe blindness caused by optic nerve and retinal injury. The experimental treatment consisted of injecting mitochondria extracted from her own leg muscles into the vitreous fluid of her eyes.

The procedure was conducted after emergency approval from the US FDA in May. It caused no inflammation or side effects, but the pupil response to light was temporary and subsided after about four weeks. The findings were posted as a preprint on 10 August, before peer review.

Co-author David Putrino said the goal was to establish safety, not efficacy, and that 'we can't prove efficacy at all, nor are we trying to.' The observed pupil response was transient, and the woman's vision was not restored.
