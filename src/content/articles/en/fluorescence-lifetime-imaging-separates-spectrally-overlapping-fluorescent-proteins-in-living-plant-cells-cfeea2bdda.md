When fluorescence microscopy is used to label several proteins at once, fluorescent proteins with similar emission colors are often not clearly separated by conventional intensity-based imaging. Researchers therefore tested whether fluorescence lifetime imaging microscopy (FLIM) with phasor plot analysis could distinguish such overlapping signals in living plant cells.

In vitro, FLIM distinguished four red fluorescent proteins—mCherry, mRFP, mApple, and tdTomato—that conventional fluorescence intensity imaging did not clearly separate. The closest pair, mCherry and mRFP, could still be told apart despite differing by only about 3 nanometers in emission wavelength and about 0.2 nanoseconds in fluorescence lifetime. In living protonemal cells of the moss Physcomitrium patens, FLIM with phasor analysis distinguished fluorescent proteins localized to the nucleus, peroxisomes, chloroplasts, and plasma membrane, using red-emitting proteins (mCherry and tagRFP-T) and green-emitting proteins (GFP and NowGFP). The analysis separated three fluorescent proteins in peroxisomes, the nucleus, and chloroplasts into distinct phasor populations and revealed an intermediate fluorescence lifetime at boundary regions between adjacent peroxisomes and chloroplasts.

The results show that fluorescence lifetime can serve as an additional dimension for separating multiple protein labels in living plant cells. The report does not give sample sizes, statistical detail, or explicit limitations of the FLIM/phasor method, so how far the approach extends beyond these demonstrations remains unspecified.
