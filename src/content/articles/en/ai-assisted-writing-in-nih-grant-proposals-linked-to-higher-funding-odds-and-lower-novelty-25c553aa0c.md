Large language model use in grant writing rose sharply after ChatGPT's public release in late 2022, according to researchers analyzing federal grant applications. Their measures showed grant writing splitting into two groups: applications with little AI assistance and those with extensive AI assistance.

In the PNAS study, proposals with stronger signs of AI-assisted writing were four percentage points more likely to receive NIH funding. At NIH, funded projects with greater AI involvement produced more follow-on publications, but not more highly cited 'hit' papers. At NSF, no significant relationship emerged between AI use and funding success or follow-on publication output.

The apparent NIH boost came with a trade-off: AI-assisted proposals tended to be less semantically distinctive and more closely aligned with ideas agencies had already funded. The researchers said they can only speculate about why NIH reviewers favored AI-assisted proposals; one possibility is that review norms reward incremental, executable projects.
