Indigenous Peruvians carried an average of 10 copies of the AMY1 gene, more copies than any of the other 83 populations examined in the study.

Using ultra-long DNA sequencing, the team found that high AMY1 copy numbers had already become common in the Andes several thousand years before Europeans arrived, ruling out the post-contact population bottleneck as the cause. The researchers estimated that beginning about 10,000 years ago, people with roughly 10 or more copies had a 1.24% survival or reproductive advantage per generation.

The per-generation advantage, though small, appears in the model to have been enough for high AMY1 copy numbers to become common before European contact. Taken together, the evidence points to selection under ancient conditions shaping the AMY1 copy-number pattern in Andean populations, rather than the later population bottleneck.
