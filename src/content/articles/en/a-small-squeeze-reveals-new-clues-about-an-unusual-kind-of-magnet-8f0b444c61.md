Iron sulfide (FeS) is a candidate altermagnet with a small magnetic moment and anomalous Hall effect. Understanding how these properties relate is limited, but applying strain offers a clean way to probe their connection.

In the experiment, researchers applied uniaxial strain to an FeS crystal and observed simultaneous reductions in both the small magnetic signal and the anomalous Hall voltage. Neutron scattering at Oak Ridge National Laboratory revealed that the underlying magnetic structure remained unchanged, but the distribution of magnetic orientations within the crystal shifted. This indicates the two effects are strongly linked and may share a common origin. The research was published in Advanced Materials (DOI: 10.1002/adma.74398).

This result provides direct experimental evidence for the coupling between magnetism and electronic transport in altermagnets. However, the article does not specify the pressure range or the quantitative reduction in magnetic moment and Hall voltage, so the practical magnitude remains to be quantified.
