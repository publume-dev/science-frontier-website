For people with overweight, conventional weight-loss methods often hinge on calorie counting or portion control, which can be hard to sustain. A secondary analysis of a randomized clinical trial explored an alternative: altering diet composition to reduce energy intake without deliberate restriction.

In the 16-week randomized trial, adults with overweight who followed an ad libitum low-fat vegan diet reduced their dietary energy density by about 30%. Participants cut daily calorie intake by roughly 357 kcal on average, despite not being instructed to limit calories. Greater reductions in energy density were associated with greater weight loss, even after accounting for changes in calorie intake.

The findings suggest that lowering dietary energy density can be an effective weight-loss strategy without relying on active calorie restriction. However, the results come from a specific diet pattern (low-fat vegan) and a 16-week period; long-term effects and generalizability still need further investigation.
