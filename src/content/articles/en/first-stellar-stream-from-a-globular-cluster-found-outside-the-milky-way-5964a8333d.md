Stellar streams are bands of stars formed when a galaxy's gravity tears apart star clusters or dwarf galaxies; previously, such streams had only been observed within the Milky Way. Analyzing archival Hubble Space Telescope data, astronomers detected a stream around the ultra-diffuse galaxy UGC 9050-Dw1 and confirmed it with ground-based telescopes.

By modeling the stream's shape, the team estimated the total mass of UGC 9050-Dw1 and determined that a large fraction of it is dark matter, consistent with previous studies. The findings were published in Nature on August 12, 2026.

This discovery offers a new tool for probing dark matter in galaxies beyond the Milky Way, but the current report does not provide quantitative values for the dark matter fraction or the galaxy's mass, nor does it discuss limitations of the method.
