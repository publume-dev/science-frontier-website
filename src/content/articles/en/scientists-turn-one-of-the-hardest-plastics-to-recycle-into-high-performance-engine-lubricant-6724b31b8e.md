Polyvinyl chloride (PVC) is one of the hardest plastics to recycle. Previously, the same team converted other plastic waste into surfactants, with work published in Science and Nature Sustainability.

Virginia Tech researchers developed a method to convert PVC into polyalphaolefin, a lubricant ingredient. The process involves heating PVC with aluminum trichloride and alpha olefins in a solvent at 158°F for three hours. The resulting product was confirmed to function as a high-performance lubricant through tests at Texas A&M University and computational analysis at Caltech. The team includes Guoliang 'Greg' Liu, Eric Munyaneza Nuwayo, Connor S. Thompson, and Abby Civiello, with economic analysis by Xi Chen.

This method offers a promising upcycling route for PVC, but the article does not specify the efficiency or yield of the conversion, nor does it provide quantitative comparisons to conventional lubricants. The scalability and economic viability of the process remain unclear, although an economic analysis is mentioned.
