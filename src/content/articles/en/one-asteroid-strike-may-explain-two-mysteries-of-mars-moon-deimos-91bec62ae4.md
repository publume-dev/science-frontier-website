Deimos, one of Mars' two moons, has surface features that have remained puzzling. Previously, scientists lacked a unified explanation for the moon's southern depression and its extensive regolith. The study used simulations with the Bern SPH (smoothed particle hydrodynamics) code and, for the first time, incorporated observations from ESA's Hera spacecraft flyby of Deimos in March 2025.

The simulations indicate that an asteroid impact about 320 meters across, striking at a 45-degree angle, could simultaneously explain the southern depression and the widespread regolith on Deimos. Additionally, the simulations suggest that Deimos's uppermost layers are exceptionally weak and its interior is highly porous, resembling rubble-pile asteroids.

This research offers a unified formation mechanism for Deimos's features and marks the first scientific use of Hera's flyby data. However, because the conclusions are based on computer simulations and observational data, other explanations for Deimos's features are still possible, and direct sampling has not been performed.
