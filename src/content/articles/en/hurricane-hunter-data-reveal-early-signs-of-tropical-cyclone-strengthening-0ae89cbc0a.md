The study, led by scientists at the University of Miami Rosenstiel School, analyzed 1,510 radar analyses from NOAA Hurricane Hunter aircraft collected during 28 hurricane seasons from 1997 through 2024, using the TC-RADAR database.

The research identified four features that favor vertical alignment of tilted tropical cyclones: a tight circulation near the sea surface, a favorably oriented tilt relative to wind shear, stronger upward motion and heavier rainfall near the low-level center, and warm ocean water, abundant atmospheric moisture, and relatively weak midlevel winds. Storms that aligned showed differences about a day beforehand, including stronger and more tightly wound low-level circulations and more widespread vigorous thunderstorms near the center, suggesting these thunderstorms may help pull the storm upright.

The findings can help forecasters recognize when a disorganized tropical cyclone is more likely to intensify, potentially providing communities more time for evacuation decisions and other preparations.
