Ketone homologation is an important transformation in organic synthesis, but classical methods such as the Tiffeneau–Demjanov and Büchner–Curtius–Schlotterbeck reactions often suffer from poor migrating-group selectivity for unsymmetrical ketones. This study introduces a new palladium-catalysed decarboxylative rearrangement to address this limitation.

The reaction enables ketone homologation via a concerted Pd(II)/Pd(IV) mechanism, forming a six-membered Pd(IV) chelate that provides precise stereochemical control. For unsymmetrical ketones, the method demonstrates higher migrating-group selectivity compared to classical approaches. It applies broadly to cyclic and acyclic ketones and aldehydes, avoids hazardous diazo reagents, and was successfully applied in the total synthesis of (+)-rupestine D.

This study offers a new ketone homologation tool with high selectivity, broad substrate scope, and the advantage of avoiding hazardous reagents. However, the report lacks quantitative data such as yields, enantiomeric ratios, or detailed substrate scope, so the practical utility remains to be fully evaluated.
