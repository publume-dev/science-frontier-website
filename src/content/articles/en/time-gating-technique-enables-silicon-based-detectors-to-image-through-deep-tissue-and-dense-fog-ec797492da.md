Scattering media such as deep tissue and dense fog cause light to scatter, making it difficult for conventional imaging techniques to see through them. Near-infrared light can penetrate such media better, but typically requires expensive detectors.

Researchers at the University of Rochester developed a lower-cost imaging system using silicon-based detectors that converts near-infrared light to visible light, overcoming scattering in deep tissue and dense fog. The method relies on a technique called time-gating with a thin film of indium tin oxide.

The significance of this technique lies in its potential to provide a more economical way to image through scattering media, though the report does not specify quantitative improvements in image quality or discuss limitations.
