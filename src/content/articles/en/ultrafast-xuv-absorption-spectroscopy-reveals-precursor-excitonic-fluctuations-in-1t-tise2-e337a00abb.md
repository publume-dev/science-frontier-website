Exciton condensation is a distinct quantum phenomenon in materials, and the nature of its precursor fluctuations has long been debated. In 1T-TiSe2, the charge-density-wave transition may be driven by exciton condensation, but direct evidence had been lacking.

The researchers used ultrafast broadband extreme-ultraviolet absorption spectroscopy (UBXAS) to measure the photoinduced response of the Se 4p states in 1T-TiSe2. They found that the response time decreases with the inverse square root of excitation fluence both below and above the phase transition, a signature of exciton dissociation via screening of the Coulomb interaction. Near Tc, the excitonic response becomes exceptionally fast even at low fluence (0.1 mJ/cm2) and is largely insensitive to excitation fluence, indicating enhanced excitonic susceptibility near the phase transition.

These results provide direct evidence that excitonic correlations persist above the transition temperature and are enhanced near Tc, supporting the idea of an exciton-driven charge-density-wave transition. However, the study is self-reported by the researchers without independent verification, though it is based on work published in Nature Physics.
