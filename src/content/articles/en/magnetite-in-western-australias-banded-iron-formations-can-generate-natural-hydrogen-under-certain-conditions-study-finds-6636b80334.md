Scientists have been exploring alternative clean energy sources, and natural hydrogen has attracted attention due to its potential environmental benefits. However, the mechanisms of natural hydrogen formation are not fully understood.

In laboratory experiments, researchers placed magnetite samples in water at 200°C under high pressure for 60 days to mimic deep underground conditions. They observed that magnetite can react with hot water to release hydrogen gas. Additionally, injecting a solution into banded iron formations can increase hydrogen generation. The study found that hydrogen production depends not only on the amount of magnetite but also on how easily water can access fresh mineral surfaces through fractures, pores, and permeable pathways.

While the article does not include quantitative results such as hydrogen yield or comparisons with other methods, and lacks details on limitations and replication status, the study highlights the critical role of rock permeability in natural hydrogen generation, providing a basis for future assessment and exploitation of natural hydrogen resources.
