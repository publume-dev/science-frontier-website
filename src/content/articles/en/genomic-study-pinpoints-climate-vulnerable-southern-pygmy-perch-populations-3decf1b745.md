To find which populations of the endangered southern pygmy perch are most at risk from future climate change, researchers combined climate modeling with genome-wide data from 467 fish at 30 sites.

The analysis found that populations in upland streams are more vulnerable to future climate change than those in lowland wetlands and rivers. Elevation alone was a strong predictor of vulnerability.

The study also reports that genetically informed captive breeding and reintroduction during the Millennium Drought preserved the restored Lower Lakes population's capacity to adapt to future climate change.
