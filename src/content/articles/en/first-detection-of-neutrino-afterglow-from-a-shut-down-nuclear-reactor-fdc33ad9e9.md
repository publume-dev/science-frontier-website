Nuclear reactors continue to emit antineutrinos after shutdown due to the decay of fission products, but this had never been directly detected before.

Using 17.2 days of data and recording about 100 candidate events, the Double Chooz collaboration detected antineutrinos from a shut-down reactor for the first time. The detected signal closely matched simulations that accounted for residual fuel inventory and decay of long-lived fission products, providing first direct confirmation of predicted antineutrino emissions from shut-down reactors and spent fuel.

This achievement suggests that detecting antineutrinos could become a new tool for monitoring shut-down reactors and spent fuel, aiding nuclear non-proliferation and safety.
