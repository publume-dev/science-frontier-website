A molecule's wavefunction describes the quantum distribution of its electrons in space, but direct imaging has been challenging, especially for molecules only nanometers in size. Traditional methods often require synchrotron radiation sources, limiting accessibility and practicality.

The team combined advanced photoelectron spectroscopy with sophisticated mathematical algorithms to reconstruct the complete molecular orbital. They used a lab-based soft-X-ray source providing ultrashort light pulses, making the technique more practical than synchrotron-based measurements. The redesigned algorithm dramatically reduces the amount of experimental data required to generate dependable three-dimensional images.

The approach could enable stroboscopic videography of wavefunctions with femtosecond resolution, allowing observation of how molecules respond to external changes. Although the report lacks specific quantitative details, the advance opens new possibilities for visualizing quantum phenomena.
