Natural DNA uses a genetic alphabet of four letters, composed of two natural base pairs.

Researchers at UC San Diego demonstrated that RNA polymerase from E. coli can accurately read and transcribe an eight-letter genetic alphabet that includes two synthetic base pairs (c1). Cryo-electron microscopy revealed structural details of how RNA polymerase recognizes the synthetic base pairs, using biochemical and structural signals similar to those for natural base pairs (c2). A related PNAS study by the same team found that RNA polymerase can recognize a pair of synthetic base pairs lacking the hydrogen bonds that normally hold DNA base pairs together (c3).

Notably, the article does not specify error rates or efficiency compared to natural DNA, so the claim of 'accurately' is qualitative (u1).
