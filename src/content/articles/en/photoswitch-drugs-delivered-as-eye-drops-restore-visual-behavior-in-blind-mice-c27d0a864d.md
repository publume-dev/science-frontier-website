Retinal degenerative diseases such as age-related macular degeneration and retinitis pigmentosa typically result in loss of photoreceptor function, leading to blindness. Current treatments are limited, and there is a lack of convenient delivery methods.

Researchers developed prosthe6, a family of photoswitchable small-molecule drugs, as substitutes for photoreceptors. The drugs target the mGlu6 protein in ON bipolar cells. In mouse models of age-related macular degeneration and retinitis pigmentosa, prosthe6 restored light-avoidance behavior; in blind zebrafish larvae, it restored saccadic eye movements (optokinetic reflex). Notably, these visual behaviors were restored both by injection into the eye and by topical administration as eye drops. The effective illumination levels were similar to those indoors or outdoors on an overcast day. The findings were published in the Journal of the American Chemical Society (JACS).

This study suggests that targeting ON bipolar cells with photoswitchable drugs could be a viable strategy for restoring visual function in retinal degeneration. However, the study only observed behavioral recovery in animal models, without quantitative effect sizes or statistical details; the duration of restored visual function and full safety profile in animals remain unclear.
