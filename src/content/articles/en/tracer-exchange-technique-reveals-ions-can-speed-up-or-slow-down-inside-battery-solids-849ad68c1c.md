Traditionally, ion diffusion in solids is assumed to follow Fick's law, where ions spread uniformly from high to low concentration. However, the applicability of this model to complex battery materials has been uncertain.

Researchers from UChicago PME, TU Delft, MIT, and UIUC developed a 'tracer exchange' technique that uses isotope tracking to observe the diffusion of lithium and sodium ions through solid lithium iron phosphate. They found both superdiffusive and subdiffusive behavior, contradicting traditional Fickian diffusion models. The study, published in Nature Communications, establishes tracer exchange as a platform for studying coupled multi-ion and electron transport in solids.

The findings suggest that ion diffusion in solids is more complex than traditional models assume, potentially impacting battery performance. The tracer exchange technique offers a new tool for future studies of ion transport in solids.
