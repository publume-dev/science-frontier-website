Researchers combined data from the ACCORD, PEACE, and SPRINT trials, totaling 20,070 participants, to assess whether high lipoprotein(a) levels are associated with cardiovascular outcomes. The analysis used Lp(a) ≥175 nmol/L as the high-risk threshold, with a median follow-up of 3.98 years.

In the pooled analysis, Lp(a) ≥175 nmol/L was associated with a 31% higher risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (HR 1.31, 95% CI 1.10–1.55), a 49% higher risk of cardiovascular death (HR 1.49, 95% CI 1.07–2.06), and a 64% higher risk of stroke (HR 1.64, 95% CI 1.14–2.37), but not with a higher risk of heart attack.

The risk was stronger among participants who already had heart disease (HR 1.30, 95% CI 1.07–1.57) than among those without existing heart disease (HR 1.18, 95% CI 0.91–1.54), where the association was not statistically significant. These findings were presented as late-breaking data at SCAI 2026, with no linked peer-reviewed publication; full model adjustments and replication status are not available from the report.
