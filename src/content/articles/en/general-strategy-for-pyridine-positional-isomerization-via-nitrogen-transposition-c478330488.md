Pyridines are ubiquitous in pharmaceuticals, agrochemicals, and materials, yet accessing specific positional isomers can be challenging. Existing methods often suffer from substrate scope limitations or lengthy synthetic sequences, making precise control of substituent positions difficult.

Researchers report a general strategy for pyridine positional isomerization via controlled reorganization of the heteroaromatic core through sequential nitrogen insertion and deletion. The method is broadly applicable to mono-, di-, and multisubstituted pyridines and operates in structurally complex molecular environments. N-atom transposition allows predictable translocation of preinstalled substituents without altering substituent identity.

This strategy offers a new route to pyridine position isomers, especially for achieving substituent position editing in complex molecular scaffolds, potentially simplifying synthetic routes in drug discovery and materials science.
