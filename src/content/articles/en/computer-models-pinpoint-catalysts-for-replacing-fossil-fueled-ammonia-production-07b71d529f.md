The conventional Haber-Bosch process currently accounts for over 90% of ammonia production and relies on fossil fuels. Electrochemical ammonia production offers a cleaner alternative, but efficient catalysts are lacking.

MIT researchers used density functional theory and machine learning to identify electronic descriptors based on N2p–metal d band hybridization that predict nitrogen reduction reaction energetics on transition metal nitrides. This approach could accelerate the search for catalysts that make electrochemical ammonia production competitive with the Haber-Bosch process. The study was published in the open-access journal EES Catalysis with DOI 10.1039/d6ey00138f.

While these computational descriptors may guide catalyst design, the study is purely theoretical; the identified catalyst candidates have not yet been tested experimentally, and the real-world impact remains uncertain.
