Bacterial biofilms are resistant to antibiotics, making chronic infections difficult to treat. Acinetobacter baumannii and Klebsiella pneumoniae are common hospital-acquired pathogens that can form biofilms.

Researchers at the Indian Institute of Science identified an enzyme, named CRhAB, from cow rumen microbes that breaks down the polysaccharide components of biofilms of Acinetobacter baumannii and Klebsiella pneumoniae. The team developed an enzyme-impregnated gauze and tested it in a mouse wound infection model, optimizing the setup to keep the dressing in place. The study was published in npj Biofilms and Microbiomes with DOI 10.1038/s41522-026-01115-3.

This finding offers a potential avenue for developing new treatments for biofilm-associated infections, though quantitative effectiveness data and comparisons with standard treatments are not provided in the article.
