Deep brain stimulation (DBS) is a common treatment for Parkinson's disease, but optimal stimulation parameters often require repeated adjustment. Previous research has mostly focused on beta oscillations in a single brain region, lacking a whole-network view of the stimulation response.

Researchers analyzed a multicenter group of 50 Parkinson's patients receiving DBS, combining spatial and temporal mapping for the first time to identify a brain network connecting the subthalamic nucleus with frontal areas. This network operates in a faster beta band (20-35 Hz), and its connection strength correlated with the degree of motor symptom improvement.

The finding suggests that connection strength in this faster beta-band network could serve as a biomarker for DBS efficacy and inform personalized stimulation parameters. However, the study is correlational, not causal, and did not report effect sizes or statistical significance, so further verification is needed.
