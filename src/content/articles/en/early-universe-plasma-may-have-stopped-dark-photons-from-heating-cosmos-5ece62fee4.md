Previously, it was thought that dark photons, a dark matter candidate, would convert into ordinary photons in the early universe, heating it and setting a mass constraint on dark photons.

A new paper in Physical Review Letters by Junwu Huang and Mohamad Shalaby of Perimeter Institute, with Anson Hook of the University of Maryland, shows that nonlinear plasma dynamics may shut off the conversion process, so dark photons would not have heated the early universe as previously thought. The finding invalidates the conventional cosmological constraint across roughly 10 orders of magnitude in mass, from about 10^-15 eV to 10^-6 eV.

The study may open up parameter space for dark photons, affecting experimental searches, but the exact magnitude and mechanism require further validation.
