Quantum heat engines are miniature engines that exploit quantum systems as working media, long explored only theoretically. Physicists have sought to realize a cyclic engine that can produce positive work repeatedly in a real quantum system.

The research team at Aalto University built a device inside a superconducting circuit consisting of a transmon qubit, a resonator, and a quantum refrigerator, operated at ultracold temperatures. The device reproduced an Otto cycle: using carefully timed control pulses, the quantum-circuit refrigerator could heat and cool the qubit on demand, allowing the engine to produce positive work repeatedly. The study was published in Nature Communications and led by Academy Professor Mikko Möttönen, with first author Tuomas Uusnäkki.

This experiment is an empirical advance in quantum thermodynamics, though the report gives no quantitative results such as efficiency or power output. The researchers suggest that the technique could one day enable autonomous readout of qubits, avoiding the need for microwave pulses traveling from millikelvin temperatures to room temperature, which might reduce the cost and complexity of quantum computers with very large numbers of qubits. This remains a future possibility, not an achieved result.
