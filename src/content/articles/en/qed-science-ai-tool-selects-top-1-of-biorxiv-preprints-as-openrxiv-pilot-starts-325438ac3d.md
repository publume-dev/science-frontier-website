QED Science is an artificial-intelligence system developed to assess the originality and validity of life-sciences manuscripts before publication. According to the article, the tool is free to use and has been used by more than 10,000 laboratories at 1,500 institutions in more than 70 countries. In November 2025, openRxiv announced that it would pilot QED Science on bioRxiv.

The system ranked 57,455 bioRxiv preprints posted between May 2025 and April 2026, selecting 574 as the top 1%. QED Science reports that, in a validation analysis of 2,879 preprints later published in peer-reviewed journals, it rated 12.9% of these papers more highly than the journals' rankings suggested, and an expert panel preferred the QED-favoured paper in 75% of decisive comparisons.

The ranking has prompted debate: some researchers call it another badge of prestige, while others question the reliability and transparency of using AI to judge scientific quality. The performance figures depend on QED Science's own analysis, which has not been independently replicated or peer-reviewed, and the article does not disclose the scoring methodology or the composition and selection of the expert panel, making independent assessment difficult.
