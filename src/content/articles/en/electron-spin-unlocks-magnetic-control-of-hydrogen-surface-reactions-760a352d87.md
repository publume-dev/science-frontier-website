In surface chemistry, the spin state of reactant molecules is often considered to have negligible influence on reactivity. However, a recent study challenges this view by showing that electron spin can serve as a control handle for surface reactions.

By controlling the spin orientation of hydrogen atoms, researchers found that when spin was parallel to a nickel surface, hydrogen adsorption was more than seven times greater than when oriented perpendicular, after accounting for background adsorption at low coverage. This spin-dependent effect disappeared on nonmagnetic copper, indicating it is linked to the surface's magnetism. In abstraction reactions on deuterium-covered nickel, hydrogen-deuterium molecule formation proceeded more efficiently with parallel spin, and the difference increased with magnetic field strength.

This finding implies electron spin can be used to control surface reactions, but its effect relies on the surface being magnetic. While the results suggest potential for spin-selective catalytic processes, the study does not clarify the mechanism of spin-surface interaction, nor does it report reaction rate constants; only differences in product amounts were observed.
