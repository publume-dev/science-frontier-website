Stable glasses are typically strong but brittle, prone to catastrophic failure under stress.

In simulations, doping a stable glass with a small fraction of self-propelled particles while shearing changes the stress-strain curve from a sharp cliff to a rounded hill, indicating later yielding at higher stress. The single shear band in brittle glass is replaced by a network of smaller bands that spread damage across the material. The study reveals an equivalence between shear rate and local active force: a rapidly sheared glass with weak activity yields like a slowly sheared glass with strong activity. The key parameter is the persistence time of active particles; short persistence times cause rattling that strengthens the glass and produces the shear-band network.

The findings are computational and await experimental validation. The approach offers a potential route to design glasses that are both strong and ductile, but exact strength improvements and optimal parameters remain to be determined.
