Paranthropus boisei is an extinct hominin species whose body size was previously inferred mainly from skeletal fossils. Its foot anatomy and gait allowed the research team to identify the species from footprints using analytical techniques first developed in 2024.

A set of fossil footprints from northern Kenya, dated to about 1.4 million years ago, records eight Paranthropus boisei individuals traveling together. The footprint sizes indicate that individuals could reach heights up to 1.8 meters and weigh around 75 kilograms, larger than earlier estimates based on skeletal remains.

The group of eight mostly adult males without females or children suggests a complex social structure, possibly involving male competition and tolerance for safety. However, the exact methods used to estimate body size from footprints and the statistical uncertainty of those estimates remain unspecified.
