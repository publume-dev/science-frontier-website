A widely publicized 2023 study claimed that scientific disruptiveness was declining, but this conclusion is now being challenged. Researchers from Vrije Universiteit Brussel and KU Leuven found that incomplete reference data account for most of the alleged decline.

In the study's largest dataset, database artifacts account for 93% of the reported decline between 1945 and 2010. Additionally, a software bug in the visualization library omitted the largest data points from published histograms without warning, though the underlying data remained included in the main analysis.

However, the Belgian team found a slight, statistically significant increase in disruption when following the guideline of papers with at least 10 references and citations, but they do not conclude that science is becoming more disruptive; the direction remains uncertain.
