Metal-organic frameworks can undergo light-induced structural changes that lead to a so-called hidden state, but the intermediate steps of this process have remained unclear. In a new study, researchers aimed to observe these steps directly.

The team used time-resolved reflectance spectroscopy with ultrashort laser pulses of six femtoseconds to track the rapid changes, combined with theoretical calculations. They observed a photoinduced hidden state forming within 30 femtoseconds. The transformation proceeds through a previously unknown intermediate electronic state, a bond-order wave state, which precedes the structural changes leading to the hidden state.

The findings reveal the pathway of hidden-state formation in a metal-organic framework and may guide the design of new light-responsive materials. The study was published in Physical Review Letters.
