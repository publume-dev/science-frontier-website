The artifacts come from Abri 122/1200, a Middle Paleolithic site in Romania. Researchers examined more than 10,000 bone fragments recovered there; no Neanderthal skeletal remains have been found at the site, so attributing the artifacts to Neanderthals rests on the site's Middle Paleolithic context rather than direct fossil evidence.

Among the fragments, 83 showed signs of intentional modification consistent with use as tools, including scraping, sharpening, wedging, and hammering. Microscopic analysis and experimental replication against modern replicas supported the archaeological identification. The tools are described as expedient, suggesting Neanderthals at the site prioritized speed and immediate usefulness over extensive shaping.

This is the first evidence that Neanderthals used bone tools in Romania. Yet because no Neanderthal skeletal remains accompany the artifacts, the identification is an archaeological inference from the Middle Paleolithic context rather than a direct biological attribution.
