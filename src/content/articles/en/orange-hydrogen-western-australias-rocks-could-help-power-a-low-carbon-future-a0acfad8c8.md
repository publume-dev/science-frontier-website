In the search for low-carbon energy, scientists have been exploring ways to produce hydrogen through natural geological processes. Researchers at CSIRO have focused on the Yilgarn Craton in Western Australia, where iron-rich mafic-ultramafic rocks may provide conditions for what is termed "orange hydrogen."

According to a study published in the International Journal of Hydrogen Energy, the CSIRO team proposed that injecting CO2-saturated water into these iron-rich rocks could both stimulate hydrogen production and mineralize carbon dioxide. Temperature was identified as a major control on hydrogen generation, with higher temperatures accelerating the reactions, but carbon mineralization was most effective between 150°C and 200°C. Lower-salinity fluids generally boosted hydrogen generation, as did alkaline conditions.

Geochemical modeling indicates that the mafic-ultramafic rocks of the Yilgarn Craton hold strong potential for supporting orange hydrogen systems. However, these findings are based on models, and laboratory experiments and field trials are required to confirm commercial-scale feasibility.
