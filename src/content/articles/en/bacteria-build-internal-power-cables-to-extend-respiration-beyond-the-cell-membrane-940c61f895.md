Conventional wisdom holds that bacterial respiration must remain confined to a membrane surface, as the electron transport chain relies on membrane structures.

A study in Nature Microbiology (DOI: 10.1038/s41564-026-02450-z) found that some bacteria build long, hollow filaments made of proteins and lipids that extend their respiratory machinery from the cell membrane into the cell interior. These filaments have a hollow interior through which electron-carrying molecules can move, effectively acting as power cables that allow many energy-processing enzymes to work beyond the cell membrane. The study was conducted by Ashleigh Kropp and colleagues.

The discovery challenges the conventional view that respiration must remain confined to a membrane surface, and related systems appear across hundreds of bacterial species, suggesting a widespread evolutionary strategy.
