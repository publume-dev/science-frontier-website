In July 2021, floods in Germany and Belgium killed 224 people across North Rhine-Westphalia, Rhineland-Palatinate, and Wallonia. Official extreme flood hazard zones are used for disaster preparedness, yet the event revealed that these maps did not capture all areas at risk.

The study found that about 50% of fatality locations in North Rhine-Westphalia and 75% in Rhineland-Palatinate were outside officially mapped extreme flood hazard zones. People aged over 60 accounted for 72% of fatalities, a significant overrepresentation. The authors recommend incorporating historical floods or worst-case scenarios into hazard mapping and improving risk communication to distinguish between damage-reducing and life-saving actions, including vertical evacuation when leaving is no longer safe.

The findings stem from a single report; full methods and uncertainty bounds are not provided in the available article. The results highlight that reliance on official hazard zones may underestimate true flood risk, but the generalizability of the recommendations requires further validation.
