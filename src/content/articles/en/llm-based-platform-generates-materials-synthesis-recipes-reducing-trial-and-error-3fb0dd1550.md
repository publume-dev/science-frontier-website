Materials synthesis often relies on trial and error. The team built a database from 4,407 open-access solid-state synthesis papers and used retrieval-augmented generation to propose recipes, achieving an average score of about 4 out of 5 when compared to reported conditions.

The platform proposed a recipe for an oxy-selenide solid electrolyte that initially yielded impurities at 600°C. After iterative refinement with experimental feedback, the team successfully synthesized a single-phase material at lower temperatures (450°C and 400°C) within a few experiments.

This closed-loop workflow, combining human experimental expertise with AI literature mining, reduces trial and error in materials synthesis and could accelerate the development of new materials.
