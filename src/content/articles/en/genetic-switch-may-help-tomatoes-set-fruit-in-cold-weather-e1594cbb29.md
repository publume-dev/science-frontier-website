Tomatoes are warm-season crops, and low temperatures can hinder normal fruit set, reducing yields. Previous research suggested roles for plant hormones and microRNAs in fruit development, but the exact mechanisms remained unclear.

Researchers identified a molecular module called miR167-ARF8 that coordinates stamen-pistil development and fruit set in tomato. By editing the SlMIR167a and SlARF8 genes, the modified plants produced seedless fruit through parthenocarpy and began fruiting earlier under all tested conditions. In winter greenhouse experiments, the edited plants produced more than 18 times as many fruits early in the season, and by harvest yielded six times more ripe tomatoes and 10 times the total weight of ripe fruit compared to unmodified plants. The edited plants were also more compact, directing more energy toward fruit production.

This research offers a potential strategy for tomato cultivation in cold environments, but commercial application requires further studies on fruit size, flavor, quality, and performance in agricultural varieties.
